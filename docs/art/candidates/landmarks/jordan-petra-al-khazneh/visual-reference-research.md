## Geometry findings used for generation

- Al-Khazneh is a tall, two-storey Nabataean façade carved directly into a near-vertical rose-sandstone cliff. It must read as one continuous rock-cut surface whose sides merge into the enclosing cliff, not as a freestanding classical building.
- The official Petra visitor authority gives a height of 39.5 m. A Department of Antiquities-hosted architectural study measures a broader formal field at approximately 42 m high by 28 m wide. Published dimensions vary with the measured boundary, so generation uses the stable visual relationship: a roughly 40 m-high façade, distinctly taller than wide, filling the terminal opening of the Siq.
- The lower order has six Corinthian-capital supports across the front: two central freestanding columns and four outer engaged or semi-circular supports. They carry a broad entablature and triangular central pediment around a tall, dark, axial doorway reached by shallow steps.
- The upper order is a three-part composition. Split or “broken” pediments flank a central circular tholos; the tholos has a columned round form and is crowned by the landmark’s prominent stone urn. This tholos-and-urn silhouette is a mandatory recognition point.
- Surviving figural and vegetal reliefs are weathered and subordinate to the architectural orders. To satisfy the clean-scene prohibition on people and animals, candidates should reduce those reliefs to faint, non-figurative eroded ornament rather than render legible bodies, faces, griffins, horses, birds, or Amazons.
- The Siq is a narrow natural gorge just over 1 km long, formed by a split in the mountain and serving as Petra’s dramatic eastern approach. At its end, close shaded canyon walls part to reveal the sunlit façade. The approach composition therefore needs tall irregular sandstone walls at both sides, a narrow stone floor, deep shade near the viewer, and a controlled opening around Al-Khazneh.
- Recognition must survive the watercolor simplification: coherent six-support lower rhythm, one central doorway, lower triangular pediment, two upper broken-pediment wings, central tholos, urn, and the façade’s direct continuity with the rock face.
