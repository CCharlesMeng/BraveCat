# Visual-reference research: Taj Mahal, Agra

Checked 2026-07-20. Remote material was consulted only to establish factual geometry, spatial relationships, and companion anatomy. No remote photograph, plan, thumbnail, or other restricted source image was downloaded or retained in this repository. The generated candidates do not reproduce a source photograph or its pixels.

## Geometry conclusions used

- The principal south-to-north axis is: main gate, quadripartite charbagh and reflecting channel, riverfront terrace with the mausoleum, the Yamuna immediately behind it, then Mehtab Bagh on the opposite bank.
- The mausoleum sits at the north end of the garden rather than at its center. A south garden view therefore places the reflecting channel in front and the Yamuna behind the platform; a north-bank view places the Yamuna in front while most of the charbagh is hidden behind the tomb.
- The white-marble tomb is a square mass with chamfered corners. Its defining elevation is one dominant bulbous double dome on a tall drum, a large central iwan, four smaller corner chhatris, and exactly four free-standing minarets at the corners of the raised square platform.
- The four platform minarets are distinct from the much smaller decorative corner pinnacles on the mausoleum. They taper gently, are capped by small chhatris, and lean subtly outward.
- The minarets frame rather than exceed the dominant central dome. The platform reads as a broad, level horizontal base beneath the tomb.
