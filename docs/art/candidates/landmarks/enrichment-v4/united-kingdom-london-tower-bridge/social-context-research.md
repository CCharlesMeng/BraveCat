## Supported facts and scene implications

### Bridge and river circulation

- Tower Bridge's official visitor material describes the high-level walkways above the road and the Thames, notes that the original walkways let pedestrians cross during bridge lifts, and says people historically preferred to wait and cross at street level. The same page explicitly describes London buses, black cabs, river boats, and cruise ships passing below the glass floors.
- Tower Bridge's official engineering history says the opening roadways were designed to preserve access to the Upper Pool of London. Vessels over 9 m tall may request a lift, river traffic has legal priority over road traffic, and the bridge opens about twice a day on average.
- Generation implication: show the bascules closed for an ordinary lived-in moment. Put pedestrians only on a footway or riverside path, generic vehicles only on the roadway, and a modest low-profile boat only on the Thames. A single unbranded red double-decker is locally credible, but no route number, operator mark, advert, plate, or other readable detail may appear. Keep all social activity sparse and outside the Portrait safeBounds.

### Night lighting

- The City of London states that its Mechanical and Electrical team maintains Tower Bridge's landmark lighting. Its street-lighting system uses LEDs and central controls that can dim or omit unnecessary light where ambient light is sufficient.
- Generation implication: use restrained operational and architectural lighting rather than a special-event colour display: a few warm lamps, low-saturation blue-grey night washes, and soft broken reflections on the Thames. Preserve a legible pedestrian ground plane and avoid neon, blown highlights, floating light orbs, and crushed black shadows.

### Plausible covered riverside rest

- London Bridge City's official Hay's Galleria page documents a nearby Grade II listed riverfront mixed-use destination with restored warehouse fabric, leisure space, and a Victorian-style glass-roofed shopping arcade. Tower Bridge's own visitor page also lists Hay's Galleria among landmarks visible from the bridge.
- Generation implication: a quiet, unbranded café/window seat or glass-roofed riverside arcade in the nearby south-bank warehouse vernacular is plausible. The exact interior and Tower Bridge sightline are an art-direction inference, not a reconstruction of a documented venue. Frame the twin towers clearly through a broad opening or window and reserve a dry, warm, low-detail bench/window-seat sleep area.
