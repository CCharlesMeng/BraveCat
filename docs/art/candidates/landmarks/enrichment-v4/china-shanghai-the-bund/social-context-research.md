## Scene-grounding conclusions

- The Bund promenade is on the west bank of the Huangpu River, facing Pudong/Lujiazui across the water. In a representative view, the river and Pudong skyline belong east of the promenade; the historic Bund building ensemble stands west of East Zhongshan No. 1 Road.
- The waterfront public realm is not one flat walkway. Its principal elements are the floodwall-top promenade, intermediate platforms and plazas, ramps, and ground-level pedestrian paths. A long, legible paved walking route and stone floodwall/rail relationship are therefore appropriate.
- The 2008 municipal design placed through traffic in the Bund tunnel and reduced the surface road to four motor-vehicle lanes plus two standby lanes for bus stops or temporary stopping. Generic cars belong only on East Zhongshan No. 1 Road, between the historic facades and the riverfront public realm—not on the promenade.
- The municipal source does not establish ordinary cycling on the floodwall-top sightseeing promenade. To avoid inventing circulation, this batch omits a cyclist rather than placing one among pedestrians.
- A modest public ferry is plausible on the Huangpu. Shanghai Ferry's Dongjin route links Dongchang Road and East Jinling Road; the Dongfu route links Dongchang Road and East Fuxing Road. The ferry should remain small, generic, unbranded, and on the river, with no route text or corporate livery.
- Ordinary night lighting should read as pale, static architectural illumination and a safely legible waterfront path. Shanghai's 2024–2035 lighting plan distinguishes routine, holiday, and major-event modes; routine mode uses restrained static light, while dynamic facades and media content belong to special modes. This batch therefore avoids lasers, light-show color changes, animated media facades, and neon spectacle.
- A sheltered rest scene is credible as a generic upper-floor historic Bund hotel lounge or river-facing window seat. The official Fairmont Peace Hotel page establishes that a century-old Bund hotel faces Pudong across the Huangpu and contains lounges and river-view hospitality spaces. The image must not reproduce a named Fairmont room, Cathay Room decor, furniture arrangement, facade trade dress, or any logo.
