## Official text findings

### Ordinary circulation

- Tower Bridge states that its high-level walkways were originally provided so pedestrians could cross while the bascules were raised, while most people preferred to wait and cross at street level. Its Glass Floors page describes London buses and black cabs on the road and river boats below. This supports mixed pedestrian, road, and river circulation, each confined to its real surface.
- Tower Bridge states that river traffic has legal priority over road traffic, that vessels over 9 m may request a lift, and that the bascules open about twice a day on average. An ordinary postcard moment may therefore show the bascules closed, light irregular traffic on the roadway, and occasional generic river craft.
- Southwark Council's Tower Bridge Conservation Area appraisal describes pedestrian access from the river embankment through an arch under Tower Bridge and identifies the west-side river wall as part of the riverside walk. This supports a broad pedestrian promenade viewpoint, not a traffic lane or fabricated platform.

Scene implications:

- Day: use a wide, dry foreground promenade Perch with readable paving perspective and direct foot contact. Keep loose pairs and solo walkers at varied depths, lateral positions, apparent scales, and directions. Keep traffic and any boat irregular, generic, and secondary.
- Night: use a readable midground riverside-path Perch outside rails and lamps. Show only sparse, naturally staggered walkers and restrained ordinary traffic; avoid a parapet row or single-file procession.
- Keep faces non-identifiable and omit livery, route numbers, advertisements, plates, signs, logos, and pseudo-text. All Ambient Life must remain outside the Portrait safeBounds.

### Access and placement

- Tower Bridge's official conditions distinguish the public right of way, pavement, street, and external bridge fabric from the ticketed attraction. The City of London has also described pedestrian crossings as maintained during major vehicle closures. These sources support ordinary pedestrian movement on the bridge and adjacent public routes.
- The Cat Perches are art-direction choices on visibly walkable riverside paving. They are not claims of exclusive access, permission to obstruct the route, or exact reconstructions of a controlled filming position.

### Rights

- Tower Bridge's official filming page says photographing or filming permanent structures such as Tower Bridge from public spaces is allowed without the structure owner's permission. It separately requires an application for commercial filming or photography inside the attraction, on Tower Bridge pavements or road, or in surrounding airspace.
- Tower Bridge's legal statement reserves intellectual-property rights in website content and restricts copying, distribution, display, and derivative use of website data or imagery beyond private non-commercial viewing.
- These official pages were consulted for text facts only. No page photograph, screenshot, map, logo, or other source asset was downloaded or retained, and no source pixels are used in the candidates.
- Public-space freedom-of-panorama language does not by itself clear the generated game artwork, branded bridge identity, transport depiction, or every jurisdictional use. Final artwork-rights, trademark/livery, source-independence, and shipping review remain required.
