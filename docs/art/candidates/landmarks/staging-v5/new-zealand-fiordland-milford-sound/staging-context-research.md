## Official-source findings

1. **New Zealand Department of Conservation / Te Papa Atawhai — “Milford Foreshore Walk”**  
   <https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-go/fiordland/places/fiordland-national-park/things-to-do/tracks/milford-foreshore-walk/>
   - DOC describes an easy 400 m loop through beech forest and along the sandy foreshore, suited to all ages and accessible to wheelchairs.
   - The walk begins at the main visitor carpark and provides some of the best views of Mitre Peak.
   - DOC also records earthquake and tsunami risk and instructs visitors to move up and away from the water after a long or strong earthquake.
   - Staging consequence: the Perch is on a broad, gently graded, public foreshore-walk viewpoint surface, visibly stable and set back above the water. It is not a mountain summit, cliff lip, narrow wilderness ledge, dock, or water-level rock.

2. **New Zealand Department of Conservation / Te Papa Atawhai — “Milford Road/Milford Sound area”**  
   <https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-go/fiordland/places/fiordland-national-park/places-to-go/milford-road-milford-sound-area/>
   - DOC describes glacial U-shaped valleys opening into rugged basins and peaks.
   - It describes Mitre Peak’s sheer rock walls as rising 1,692 m directly from the water and again directs visitors to the Foreshore Walk for strong views.
   - Staging consequence: the Scene preserves the steep asymmetric summit, direct mountain-to-water relationship, enclosing glacial walls, calm long water, and low native vegetation. The public foreground stays subordinate to the fjord.

3. **Te Rūnanga o Ngāi Tahu — “Reimagining Piopiotahi”**  
   <https://ngaitahu.iwi.nz/opportunities-and-resources/publications/te-karaka/reimagining-piopiotahi/>
   - Te Rūnanga identifies Piopiotahi as a treasured ancestral place within Te Rua-o-te-Moko and records eight Papatipu Rūnaka exercising mana whenua in the region.
   - The article records multiple accounts associated with the name Piopiotahi rather than one uncontested origin.
   - Staging consequence: prefer `Piopiotahi / Milford Sound` pending product-wide cultural review. Do not turn creation narratives, waka, adze, pou, pounamu, place names, or generic “Māori-inspired” motifs into decoration without mana-whenua-led direction.

## Staging decisions

- The new variant uses a generalized compacted foreshore-walk viewing pad continuous with an easy path. It does not claim to reproduce a precise built platform or named facility.
- The viewpoint is broad, dry, gently graded, visibly supported, set back from the shoreline, and free of stairs, cliff edges, narrow ridges, docks, roads, rails, signs, or foreground occlusion.
- The distant `gaze` Perch is on the public path surface, with the small Portrait secondary to Mitre Peak, the fjord, and the enclosing walls.
- This is a nature Scene, so Ambient Life people/vehicle grouping is not applicable. No people, vehicles, boats, buildings, or animals are painted into the Scene.
- The Scene remains Cat-free. Approved Minho appears only on a separate non-final placement-preview sheet.

## Conservation, cultural, and access warning

DOC’s Foreshore Walk page states “No dogs” and points other pets to conservation-land rules. This research does not establish that a real cat may access the viewpoint, and the fictional Cat preview must not encourage visitors to bring cats or other pets into sensitive conservation habitat. The depicted support is physically human-accessible; animal-access permission remains explicitly unestablished. Human cultural, conservation, destination, and composite review is required before any promotion.

The area also carries real earthquake and tsunami risk. The composition’s setback above ordinary water level is visual staging, not a safety guarantee or evacuation claim.
