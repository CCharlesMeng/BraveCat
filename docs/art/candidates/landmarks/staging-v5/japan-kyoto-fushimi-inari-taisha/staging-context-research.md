# Fushimi Inari Taisha staging-context research

Research date: 2026-07-20  
Use: staging-v5 candidate direction for `japan-kyoto-fushimi-inari-taisha`  
Method: official text sources only. No source photograph, map, logo, page layout, or other external visual asset was downloaded, attached to generation, copied, or retained.

## Official-source findings

1. **Fushimi Inari Taisha — “Requests from Fushimi Inari Taisha”**  
   <https://inari.jp/en/request/>
   - The shrine describes its grounds as a sacred area and active place of worship.
   - It prohibits entering restricted buildings or fenced areas, damaging torii or other property, putting objects on lanterns, disturbing worshippers, blocking narrow paths for photography, approaching wildlife, bringing pets other than necessary assistance animals, and commercial photography without permission.
   - Staging consequence: visitors remain few, quiet, non-identifiable, and on the open walking route. They do not stop for selfies, block circulation, touch gates or lanterns, enter restricted structures, or perform invented rituals. No vehicle or animal is painted into either Scene.

2. **Kyoto City Tourism Association — “Fushimi Inari Taisha Shrine”**  
   <https://kyoto.travel/en/destinations/fushimi-inaritaisha-shrine/>
   - Kyoto’s official tourism site describes a long torii route winding through the hills and identifies the upper-precinct walk as a day hike.
   - It explicitly discusses walking in the late afternoon and early evening.
   - Staging consequence: daytime and restrained early-night pedestrian circulation are factually supportable. The night Scene uses open blue-gray ambient shadows and modest fixed-path light, not a claimed festival illumination, horror treatment, or midnight access promise.

3. **Kyoto City Tourism Association — “Comfortable access to Fushimi (Fushimi Inari Taisha Shrine)”**  
   <https://kyoto.travel/en/getting-around/comfortable-access-to-fushimi-fushimi-inari-taisha-shrine/>
   - JR Inari Station opens immediately toward the large torii and main approach. From Keihan Fushimi-inari Station, visitors walk east through the approach area and cross the bridge and rail line before reaching the shrine.
   - Staging consequence: pedestrian movement on the stone approach is ordinary and credible. Vehicles do not belong on the depicted torii path.

## Staging decisions

- Both revisions depict an offset pair plus isolated walkers rather than a centered or single-file procession.
- The figures vary in lateral position, depth, apparent scale, travel direction, stride, and gesture. Mild natural overlap is limited to the paired relationship; figures remain secondary to the torii rhythm.
- Day Perch: a dry, visible midground stone approach plane, clear of gate posts, visitors, plants, rails, hard shadows, and foreground occlusion.
- Night Perch: a distant readable stone step under the torii rhythm, clear of travelers, lamps, posts, and foreground occlusion.
- Torii surfaces remain blank. This is a product constraint against readable or pseudo-text, not a claim that real torii lack donor inscriptions.
- Scene files remain Cat-free. The approved Minho composite is a non-final placement preview only.

## Cultural and access warning

The shrine’s official visitor rules prohibit ordinary pets. A fictional Cat placement must not be represented as evidence that bringing a cat onto the shrine grounds is permitted or culturally appropriate. The Scene’s physical support and perspective can pass art-placement QA while real-world animal-access plausibility remains explicitly unestablished. Promotion therefore requires shrine-specific cultural and destination review.
