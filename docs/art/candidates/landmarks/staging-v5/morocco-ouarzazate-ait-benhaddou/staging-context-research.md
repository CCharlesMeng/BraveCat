## Heritage and geometry findings

- UNESCO describes Aït Ben Haddou as a **ksar**: a collective group of earthen dwellings inside high defensive walls reinforced by angle towers, not one palace or a generic kasbah.
- The property sits in the Ounila Valley at the southern High Atlas foothills. Its compact houses climb toward a summit loft/granary; community areas include a public square and grain-threshing areas outside the ramparts.
- UNESCO identifies the place as a traditional pre-Saharan habitat whose earthen construction, configuration, materials, natural setting, and social setting are integral to its authenticity. It is living heritage, not an abandoned fantasy ruin or merely a film set.
- The Moroccan National Tourist Office independently describes the ksar in the Ounila Valley, with buildings flanking the hill up to an old summit granary.

## Access and protection findings

- A UNESCO-hosted management document records selection of a bridge intended exclusively for pedestrians between the riverbanks. Its stated purposes included easier access for residents, tourists, and visitors. This supports a connected visitor approach on ordinary ground; it does not establish public access to roofs, wall tops, fragile terraces, or protected masonry.
- UNESCO conservation reporting identifies uncontrolled visitor pressure and damage as management concerns and calls for visitor routing/control. The World Heritage Committee’s 2024 decision again encouraged a visitor-management plan and a development plan.
- The broad overlook/approach in this candidate is therefore outside the protected ksar, connected to a plausible pedestrian route, and safely set back from drops. It must not be read as permission to stand on an earthen roof, defensive wall, ruin edge, cliff, or conservation-sensitive surface.
