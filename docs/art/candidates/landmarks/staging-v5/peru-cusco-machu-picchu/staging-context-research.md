# Machupicchu staging-v5 context research

Research date: 2026-07-20  
Destination: `peru-cusco-machu-picchu` (Machupicchu / 马丘比丘)  
Use: factual visitor circulation, conservation, access, photography, and rights planning only

## Staging conclusions

- The Ministry of Culture's current visitor system has three circuits grouping ten routes. Circuit 1 is the Panoramic circuit; Route 1-B is the Upper Terrace route. This supports a high, route-based panoramic viewpoint in which the citadel and mountains dominate.
- Visitors must remain on their established circuit and route. The candidate therefore places all incidental visitors on one broad natural earth-and-grass route and keeps them off archaeological masonry, agricultural terrace tops, walls, and cliff edges.
- The Perch is on stable packed natural ground with a low natural bank and visible route continuation. It is not protected masonry, a narrow wall top, a terrace-wall crown, or a cliff lip.
- Official conduct rules prohibit climbing or leaning on structures, moving stone, leaving the route, drones, stabilizing equipment, and advertising-purpose filming or photography. The scene omits all such conduct and equipment.
- The 2026–2031 Master Plan was approved by Ministerial Resolution No. 075-2026-MC, reinforcing that conservation and controlled visitor management remain active policy rather than merely historical context.

## Material access warning

- The official code of conduct prohibits visitors from entering with animals, except guide dogs. The selected ground is physically stable and lies on a plausible visitor route, but a live companion cat would not be legally admitted under the published rule.
- The Portrait composite is fictional QA for a non-shipping game candidate, not documentation of permitted real-world animal access. Human cultural/access review must decide whether this narrative depiction is acceptable or whether a non-monument, outside-controlled-zone overlook is required before promotion.
- This limitation is recorded explicitly rather than treating spatial support as proof of legal access.
