# Visual reference research: Aït Ben Haddou

Checked 2026-07-20. The four primary/official sources below were consulted as text for factual geometry, landscape, and conservation context. No remote photograph, video frame, map image, or other source-media file was downloaded, retained, or supplied to image generation.

## Geometry and setting cues used

- Read the subject as a **ksar**: a compact collective group of earthen dwellings inside high defensive walls, not a single kasbah and not a generic desert palace.
- Preserve the stepped, hill-climbing silhouette: clustered lower dwellings rise toward the summit granary/loft, with a clear crown against the sky.
- Show high outer walls reinforced by angular corner towers. Some larger houses resemble small urban castles, with tall angle towers and restrained clay-brick decoration near their upper sections.
- Keep tower tops and parapets varied but coherent, with small crenellated or toothed profiles rather than fantasy battlements.
- Use earthen massing and muted ochre-brown surfaces consistent with rammed earth/adobe and wood construction. Avoid polished stone, red-brick city walls, oversized domes, minarets, or invented monumental gates.
- Set the ksar in the Ounila Valley at the southern High Atlas foothills: a sparse, broad oued/riverbed foreground with restrained scrub or cultivated green traces, backed by dry layered hills. The natural and built colors should remain closely related.
- Keep the composition legible at postcard scale: the compact fortified cluster, corner towers, stepped ascent, summit crown, and sparse valley floor are the recognition points.

## Living-heritage and conservation cautions

- Aït Ben Haddou is a culturally significant traditional habitat, not an abandoned fantasy ruin or merely a film set. Do not invent collapse, romanticize decay, or depict residents as exotic decoration.
- UNESCO identifies socio-economic and cultural change, reduced maintenance, and abandonment as vulnerabilities. Its 2024 state-of-conservation account also reports declining permanent residential occupation alongside growth in visitor-oriented commerce and guest houses.
- The September 2023 Al Haouz earthquake affected several structures and existing ruins; CERKAS assessed damage and post-earthquake stabilization/restoration. A Moroccan government update dated 2026-06-29 reported restoration and rehabilitation still in progress.
- Candidate art therefore avoids asserting an exact current restoration state, newly rebuilt detail, damage pattern, visitor infrastructure, or commercial signage. It depicts a generalized, intact heritage ensemble based on stable defining geometry.
