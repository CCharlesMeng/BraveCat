# Visual reference research: Siem Reap Angkor Wat

Checked 2026-07-20. The sources below were consulted as text for factual geometry, landscape context, species plausibility, conservation sensitivity, and rights boundaries. No remote source photograph, gallery image, map, PDF image, screenshot, or copied pixel was downloaded into the repository, supplied to the image generator, traced, photobashed, or retained.

## Geometry and setting cues used

- Read Angkor Wat as a broad west-facing temple-mountain within a strongly horizontal landscape, not as a single isolated tower.
- Preserve a five-tower quincunx: one tallest central lotus-bud sanctuary tower and four lower corner towers, with all five countable in a gently elevated, slightly oblique frontal view.
- Keep the central sanctuary above nested, receding terraces and long horizontal galleries. Lower gopura, colonnades, and gallery rooflines should remain subordinate to the five-tower crown.
- Preserve the west approach as a straight sandstone causeway crossing the wide moat and leading axially toward the entrance and central sanctuary. APSARA describes the west causeway as about 200 m long and 12 m wide.
- Keep water geometry plausible: a broad enclosing moat and/or near-west reflecting basin with calm reflections, low banks, restrained lotus growth, and no invented cliffs or mountain backdrop.
- Use a low tropical tree line around the monument. UNESCO describes the wider archaeological park as partly forested and rich in hydraulic structures, routes, and reservoirs; vegetation must frame rather than engulf the principal silhouette.
- Stone should read as weathered warm gray-brown sandstone with subdued moss/sage accents, not saturated orange masonry.
- Reserve a quiet, low-detail, unmarked Portrait-safe area at bottom center on open causeway or low bank. It must not cover the axial causeway edges, tower silhouettes, galleries, reflections needed for recognition, or the rare Companion.

## Companion evidence and limits

- The planned Companion is exactly one long-tailed macaque (`Macaca fascicularis`), painted directly into the rare scene only.
- IUCN records the species as native and extant in Cambodia, occurring through most of Cambodia. It also records long-tailed macaques as generalists commonly found in human-altered habitats including temples and roadsides. Those facts make a small macaque at a Cambodian temple setting ecologically plausible.
- This research did **not** locate an APSARA or UNESCO statement documenting a specific wild long-tailed macaque at Angkor Wat itself. The evidence supports regional and habitat plausibility, not a site-specific occurrence claim.
- IUCN assessed the species as Endangered in 2022, with a decreasing population, and notes that it is normally protected in Cambodia. Depict it neutrally and at a distance: one small, anatomically credible gray-brown macaque seated on a lateral stone, long tail visible, no food, feeding, handling, crowd interaction, performance, collar, clothing, or comic behavior.
- The v3 plan caps Companion height at 6% of the scene. Keep the macaque visually secondary, outside Portrait `safeBounds`, away from tower/gallery recognition points, and clearly a scene-only non-player Companion.
