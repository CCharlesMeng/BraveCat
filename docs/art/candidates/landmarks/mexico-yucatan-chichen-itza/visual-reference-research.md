## Geometry and setting cues

- El Castillo (the Temple of Kukulkán) must read as a square stepped pyramid: nine receding terrace bodies with inclined faces, panel rhythm, and a broad axial stairway on each of the four sides.
- The north stair is the principal face. Its flanking balustrades terminate in serpent heads at ground level; at postcard scale these should remain restrained stone masses rather than invented ornament.
- A compact square temple crowns the pyramid. INAH describes four entrances and a north-facing principal facade, so the summit must not become a pointed cap, dome, tower, or open pavilion.
- The monument stands on the broad, open Great Leveling/terrace system. The scene should therefore retain a quiet pale limestone-and-short-grass plaza around the pyramid, with only a low subtropical tree line and subdued archaeological context.
- Recognition points are the square overall mass, nine clearly stepped terraces, central stair geometry, square summit temple, and open plaza separation. Avoid crowding these with secondary monuments, dense jungle, dramatic mountains, or an invented city skyline.
- The golden-hour variant may use long low shadows, but must not invent or claim the equinox “descending serpent” effect.

## Companion plausibility

- The planned companion is the Yucatan Jay, `Cyanocorax yucatanicus` (Corvidae).
- Cornell Lab’s Birds of the World describes it as a black-and-blue jay of deciduous forests across the Yucatán Peninsula and adjacent Belize and Guatemala. An adult black-and-blue bird is therefore regionally plausible near the site’s low forest edge.
- The cited sources establish regional habitat plausibility, not a documented observation on El Castillo’s plaza. Treat the rare scene as a quiet plausible encounter, not a site-occurrence claim.
- Use exactly one tiny adult jay on a lateral low stone: black head and underparts with blue back/wings/tail, compact corvid silhouette, no flock and no other animal. It must remain at or below 5% of frame height, outside `safeBounds`, and secondary to the pyramid.
