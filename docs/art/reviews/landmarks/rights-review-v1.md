Review date and source access date: **2026-07-20**  
Scope: **20 destinations / 48 active generated scenes**  
Decision: **not cleared for shipping**

> This is a conservative product-risk review, not legal advice. It identifies evidence, uncertainties, and practical release gates; qualified counsel should decide disputed questions of copyright exceptions, adaptation, trademark, cultural-property rules, and territorial exposure.

## Method and risk labels

This review separately considers: **(1)** rights in source photographs and other references; **(2)** whether a generated output could legally be an adaptation even without copied pixels; **(3)** copyright, freedom-of-panorama, cultural-property, and site rules affecting the depicted subject; **(4)** trademark, statutory-symbol, brand, and false-association concerns; and **(5)** a practical shipping decision. Creative Commons itself states that facts and ideas are not protected but their expression can be, and whether a modification is an adaptation depends on applicable law. ([Creative Commons copyright basics](https://creativecommons.org/course/cc-cert-edu/unit-2-copyright-law/2-1-copyright-basics/); [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); accessed 2026-07-20)

- **Low** — no destination-specific blocker found; clear after the common provenance gate, visual similarity review, and ordinary trademark hygiene.
- **Medium** — a documented license, attribution, site-policy, or source-log issue has a concrete compliance path.
- **High** — do not ship until targeted counsel resolves a material legal-scope question; permission may or may not ultimately be required.
- **Blocked** — a first-party rule presently calls for permission/licensing, prohibits the intended category of use, or leaves an unacceptable conflict with another rule.

The labels are product recommendations, not conclusions that a use is lawful or unlawful. Trademark rights are separate from copyright and may protect words, drawings, symbols, shapes, or other source-identifying signs; no comprehensive national or international trademark search was performed. ([WIPO trademark overview](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/trademarks); accessed 2026-07-20)

## Common gates for all 48 scenes

### 1. Input and generation provenance

All 48 records identify image generation through Cursor/GenerateImage, list the same repository style references, and state that the model and seed were unavailable; the manifests do not record who made or licensed the style references. Before shipping, preserve a dated copy of the applicable Cursor terms, document the account/plan used, identify any underlying image provider if Cursor can supply it, and attach ownership or license evidence for each style reference. ([candidate manifest](../../candidates/landmarks/manifest.candidates.v2.json); [Cursor Terms of Service](https://cursor.com/terms-of-service); accessed 2026-07-20)

Cursor's terms require the user to hold the permissions needed for submitted inputs and assign only Anysphere's rights, if any, in suggestions. The U.S. Copyright Office separately says prompt-only AI output is not protectable in the United States unless sufficient human-authored expression, selection, arrangement, or modification is present; that copyrightability question does not answer whether an output infringes someone else's work. ([Cursor Terms of Service](https://cursor.com/terms-of-service); [U.S. Copyright Office AI report, Part 2](https://copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf); accessed 2026-07-20)

**Required remediation:** obtain a signed style-reference provenance declaration; retain prompts, generation timestamps, rejected-attempt logs, and human-edit records; run reverse-image/similarity review on every final PNG; and record who selected, cropped, composited, and approved each scene. These steps answer gaps visible in the manifests and support a human-authorship record without claiming that documentation alone defeats an infringement allegation. ([candidate manifest](../../candidates/landmarks/manifest.candidates.v2.json); [U.S. Copyright Office AI report, Part 2](https://copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf); accessed 2026-07-20)

### 2. Open-license handling

CC BY 2.0 permits commercial sharing and adaptation but requires appropriate credit, a license link, and change indication; CC BY-SA 3.0/4.0 adds ShareAlike when an adaptation is shared. Wikimedia warns that file-page licensing is contributor-supplied and carries no warranty, so the file page and license evidence should be archived at clearance time. ([CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/); [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/); [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); [Wikimedia reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia); accessed 2026-07-20)

“Geometry only” and “no pixels copied” reduce risk but do not by themselves prove that an output is not an adaptation of a photograph's protected composition or other expression. If counsel treats a scene as Adapted Material, license and mark that scene file—not necessarily unrelated game code or merely aggregated assets—under the required same or compatible ShareAlike terms. ([Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [Creative Commons ShareAlike interpretation](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/ShareAlike_interpretation); accessed 2026-07-20)

### 3. Site-capture rules versus downstream depiction rights

Rules requiring permits for an on-site commercial shoot do not automatically establish ownership of an independently generated depiction made without a site visit or source photograph. This review treats such rules as context unless the operator also claims downstream image, brand, symbol, or reproduction rights—as STB, Sydney Opera House, the Colosseum park, the Christ Sanctuary, and Peru's Machu Picchu authority do. (Examples: [Tower Bridge public-space guidance](https://www.towerbridge.org.uk/filming-and-photography), [ASI monument guidance](https://asi.nic.in/pages/Monuments), [SANParks filming policy](https://www.sanparks.org/parks/table-mountain/filming-photography-events), [STB policy](https://www.stb.gov.sg/about-stb/stb-brand-assets/); accessed 2026-07-20)

### 4. Release controls

Until each gate closes, keep `shippingEligible=false`, exclude blocked/high scenes from store art, trailers, ads, social posts, merchandising, downloadable press kits, and paid acquisition, and do not describe any scene as “official,” “licensed,” or operator-approved. Trademark risk turns on likely source, sponsorship, or approval confusion and is use-context specific. ([production manifest](../../production/landmarks/manifest.v1.json); [USPTO likelihood-of-confusion guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion); accessed 2026-07-20)
